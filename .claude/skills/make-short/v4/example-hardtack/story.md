## Facts (checked 2026-11-16)

**TRUE, with sources:**
- Soldiers broke hardtack up "with the butt of their rifles". They called it "tooth duller", "sheet iron crackers" and "worm castles". They softened it in coffee, which also let them skim off the maggots and worms. Civil War hardtack is still held in museums today. Source: [NPS Manassas](https://www.nps.gov/mana/learn/kidsyouth/hardtack-during-the-civil-war.htm).
- Weevils floating in the coffee and being skimmed off. Source: John D. Billings, *Hardtack and Coffee* (1887).
- The ration was 1 lb of hard bread a day (Revised Regulations of 1861), and 1 lb of hard bread was the march ration. Sources: [Wikipedia, Foods of the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foods_of_the_American_Civil_War), [essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com](https://essentialcivilwarcurriculum.com/federal-army-rations.html).
- Each cracker was about 3 in square and ½ in thick, about ten to the pound. Source: WVTF / Virginia Tech history professor.
- Baked hard and dry, hardtack stays intact for years. Source: [Wikipedia, Hardtack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtack).
- Mould needs moisture (general food science). Dry pasta keeps for years (everyday fact).

**FABLE (invented but obviously archetypal):** the recruit, his tooth, the old hands' joke, the cook's trade, and the loaf scene.

**Avoided as unverified:**
- the "16 holes" count (a modern convention: the props show holes but the line never says a number);
- leftover Mexican-War hardtack;
- "baked four times" (that was ship's biscuit).
